# Domain Research: Christian Discipleship and Teaching Publication

## Executive Summary

The domain for Kovasz is not just “Christian content.” It is discipleship formation: helping new believers understand Scripture, helping mature believers deepen study, and connecting theology to daily Christian life.

## Domain Themes Observed

### Scripture-first content

- The article should start from a biblical text or theological claim, then move to interpretation, application, and reflection.
- Scripture blocks, cross references, and study notes should be core, not decorative.

### Dual audience lanes

- New believers need guidance, explanations, and a “start here” entry path.
- Mature believers and leaders need study aids, deeper series, and practical ministry resources.
- The split should be handled mainly by filters and series rather than by a hard landing-page fork.

### Topic clusters that matter

- Pastoral Theology
- Christian Life and Personal Growth
- Marriage, Family, and Relationships
- Ethics

These are strong content families because they map to daily Christian formation rather than abstract theology alone.

### Content formats that fit the domain

- educational articles
- devotionals
- inspiring stories
- testimonies and experiences
- study aids
- translated resources

### Trust and positioning

- About/mission/doctrine pages matter more here than in a standard blog.
- The site needs theological clarity, not just aesthetic polish.
- Readers should understand what the publication is for and what kind of teaching posture it has.

## Domain Pattern from Comparables

Desiring God is highly formation-oriented and heavily resource-branded. The Gospel Coalition is broader, more editorially expansive, and more taxonomy-driven. Kovasz can combine those strengths in a narrower, Hungarian, pastoral publication.

## Content Model Implications

1. Series should be treated as teaching journeys, not just article collections.
2. Resources should function as a practical study library.
3. About should mix mission, manifesto, and faith posture.
4. The homepage should lead with a flagship article but still expose the formation ecosystem.
5. The article page should remain calm and readable while still foregrounding Scripture and study aids.

## Open Domain Questions

- What theological statement or doctrinal posture will govern the publication?
- How explicit should the site be about denominational boundaries, if any?
- Should the new-believer path be a visible named journey or mostly implied through series labels?
- Should translated content be clearly labeled as translations, adapted summaries, or curated republications?
